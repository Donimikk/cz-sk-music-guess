# Naming research (2026-10-21)

Working name so far: **Hudle**. This file records how alternatives were generated and checked.

## Method
Followed the practitioner process in [raw.studio – brand naming process](https://raw.studio/blog/brand-naming-process):
brief → generate in rounds → score each name 1–5 on fixed criteria → clear domains and trademarks → test finalists
with real users (unprompted recall, cold pronunciation, native-speaker cultural scan). Its benchmarks (60–120 ideas,
7/10 recall, 15–25 testers per market) are the agency's own, not studies.

The "-dle" suffix is an informal convention meaning "a game in the Wordle family", not a rule
([Surrey Morphology Group](https://morph.surrey.ac.uk/index.php/2022/03/09/word-games/),
[Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=57679)).

## Brief
- Positioning: daily game, guess a Czech/Slovak song from its first seconds.
- Audience: CZ + SK, mostly 15–35, rap and pop listeners.
- Territory: playful, local, quick.
- Must: reads the same in Czech and Slovak; domain without diacritics; short.
- Off-limits: "spotle", "songspot", "heardle" (hub decision); Spotify/Apple marks.

## Existing uses found
- **Hudle** is an established Indian sports-venue booking app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1380053168)).
  Different trademark class, but it owns the name in app stores and search.
- No Czech or Slovak daily song-guessing game found in a web search (only Heardle revivals and niche clones).
- "Hitodle", "Pesnidle", "Hudbadle", "Bstrana": no web results.

## Scorecard (1–5, my scores, to be redone by the user and testers)
Criteria from the raw.studio list, condensed: memorable, pronounceable in CZ and SK, distinct, spelled after hearing it,
fits the game, domain, conflict risk, cultural safety.

| Hudbadle | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | ? | 31 + ? |
| Hudle | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 2 | ? | 27 + ? |
| Uhádni | 4 | 5 | 1 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 2 | 5 | 30 |
| Pesnidle / Písnidle | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | ? | 27 + ? |
| B-strana | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 30 |

"?" = needs a native-speaker check (e.g. whether "Hudle" evokes a slang word in Czech). Not verified.
"Hudba" means music in both Czech and Slovak; "hit" is used in both, which is why Hitodle and Hudbadle score high on CZ+SK.
"Uhádni" is generic (weak as a mark, hard to find in search). "B-strana" fits the record motif but a B-side means
lesser-known songs, the opposite of a hit game.
